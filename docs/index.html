The problem

Michigan lets online sportsbooks subtract the free bets they hand out before tax is figured. The tax or payment to the State is 8.4% of what is left, so a sportsbook that gives away enough free play in a month can owe nothing. This page, written for the Michigan Gaming Control Board, shows how much free play is deducted, who deducts the most, and how often a sportsbook pays the State nothing.

Descriptive
The Michigan Gaming Control Board needs to see how much free play the state's online sportsbooks deducted for every $100 wagered, and how often a sportsbook paid no state tax in a month, across the 15 licensed operators, commercial and tribal, month by month from January 2021 to October 2026, to learn which sportsbooks lean hardest on free-bet promotions and whether that reliance is fading.
Predictive
At the start of each month, using only what each sportsbook reported in earlier months, the Board wants two estimates for that sportsbook's month ahead: the free play it will deduct per $100 wagered (a percent), and whether it will pay no state tax that month (yes or no), so that staff can review the free-play claims of the flagged sportsbooks first.
Prescriptive
Given those estimates, what cap on the monthly free-play deduction, as a percent of a sportsbook's gross sports betting receipts, should the Board recommend that the Legislature write into law, to collect the most state and city tax, with one cap for every operator and no cap below the share of gross receipts that the median sportsbook-month actually deducted?

This report takes up the first two. The third is not solved here.

The data in brief

The data are the Board's own "Internet Sports Betting Revenues and Taxes" workbooks, one for each year from 2021 to 2026, downloaded by hand on 23 November 2026. One row is one licensed operator in one month: 3 commercial Detroit casinos and 12 tribal operators, January 2021 to October 2026.

The workbooks hold 1,956 operator-month cells. Removing 888 duplicate copies, 30 rows for months not yet reported, 114 months with no wagering and 1 closing correction leaves 923 sportsbook-months. No row was removed for being extreme and no missing value was filled in. 21 months with a deduction larger than a quarter of the dollars wagered are flagged as lump deductions, and each operator's first, partial month (15 in all) is flagged too.

Free play is not a published column. It is gross receipts minus adjusted gross receipts, the deduction the law allows, in the month it was claimed. Michigan's site terms do not allow the workbooks to be republished, so the repository holds the download steps and a checksum for each file.

Findings

How much free play is deducted?

The median sportsbook-month deducted $3.71 of free play for every $100 wagered. Half of all months fall between $1.90 and $5.53; 81 months (8.81%) reach $10 or more, and the largest is $121.84. I expected two clusters; the only sign of a second group is 122 months under $1.

No state tax or payment was made in 449 of the 923 sportsbook-months (48.65%). Asked what I would have guessed before seeing any number, I said under 10%.

Who are the sportsbooks, and which deduct the most?

Three sportsbooks took 77.22% of all dollars wagered: FanDuel (33.48%), DraftKings (27.46%) and BetMGM (16.29%). The nine smallest took 5.70% together. I expected the top three to hold 50% to 70%.

The biggest brands are not the heaviest users of free play per $100. The two highest medians belong to smaller tribal sportsbooks, Lac Vieux Desert (Fanatics) at $6.34 and Hannahville (Hard Rock Bet) at $5.13. FanDuel ($3.83) and DraftKings ($3.54) sit beside the overall median. I expected the big brands to deduct more.

Is reliance on free play fading?

The yearly median fell from $5.79 per $100 in 2021 to $3.39 in 2023 and $3.09 in 2026. I expected it to rise. The drop comes from tribal sportsbooks getting older: $9.05 per $100 in their first six months, $3.97 in the second year and $2.52 after four years. Commercial sportsbooks stayed between $3.36 and $4.15 at every age, though their first two age bands hold only 18 months each.

Taking all months together the two types are close: a median of $3.88 for commercial sportsbooks and $3.65 for tribal ones. Size tells little: the correlation between free play per $100 and the size of the month is -0.07. I expected commercial sportsbooks and bigger months to show more.

How often is no tax paid, and by whom?

Tribal sportsbooks paid nothing to the State in 56.66% of their months, commercial ones in 21.43%, as I expected. For tribal sportsbooks the share fell from 73.95% in 2021 to 19.59% in 2026. For commercial sportsbooks it swung, reaching 55.56% in 2024. I expected the share to rise.

Do the unusual months change the picture?

Leaving out the lump-deduction months and the first months moves the overall median only from $3.71 to $3.65. The mean falls from $5.18 to $4.17 and the 2021 median from $5.79 to $4.66, because most of the flagged months are in 2021. I expected no change at all. The explorer below lets you leave them out.

Which variables move together?

Among things known before a month starts, free play per $100 is most closely related to its own value the month before (0.59), and paying no tax to paying none the month before (0.79) and to the size of the previous month (-0.46). My guess was that size would matter most for free play. A correlation shows that two things move together in a straight line; it does not show that one causes the other.

The two models

Both models are ordinary linear regressions fitted to every row with the needed values and judged on those same rows. Each uses three predictors known before the month starts. I recorded the direction I expected for each slope before fitting.

Model 1: free play per $100 wagered

free play per $100 = 3.2356 + 0.4975 × (last month's free play per $100) − 0.0323 × (months active) + 0.1308 × (commercial)

A sportsbook that deducted $1 more per $100 last month tends to deduct about 50 cents more this month, holding the other predictors fixed; I expected less. Each extra month of age goes with about 3 cents less; I expected more. The model does not tell commercial and tribal sportsbooks apart. It accounts for 35.7% of the differences, with a typical miss of $5.46 per $100 across 900 months. 94% of predictions land within $5, but 18 months came in more than $10 above the prediction, and 12 of those are lump-deduction months.

Model 2: the probability of paying no state tax

probability of no tax = 0.6532 + 0.7353 × (paid no tax last month) − 0.0771 × (log of last month's dollars wagered) − 0.0084 × (commercial)

A sportsbook that paid no tax last month has a predicted probability of paying none this month about 73.5 percentage points higher, holding the other predictors fixed. Each tenfold increase in last month's wagering goes with about 7.7 percentage points lower. Both are the directions I expected. Once those two are held fixed the model shows no difference between commercial and tribal sportsbooks.

Calling 0.5 or more a yes, the model is right for 813 of 906 months (89.7%). Always guessing that tax was paid would be right 51.8% of the time. 5 predictions (0.55%) fall outside 0 to 1 and are shown as they are.

Limitations and next steps

  • Coverage. Internet sports betting in Michigan only. Retail betting and internet casino games are not here, and 2026 stops in October.
  • Who is missing. There are no bettors in these data. One row is a sportsbook's month, so nothing here says who received free bets or whether anyone was harmed.
  • Measurement. Free play is the deduction claimed for tax in a month, and it can be claimed late. The publisher does not define "Total Handle". Figures are as reported by operators, and not all are audited.
  • What description cannot establish. Every result is an association. Almost every operator started in 2021, so age and calendar time can hardly be separated.
  • The models. Each was judged on the rows it was fitted to, so its fit is a best case.

What the Board could do next. Review first the free-play claims of small sportsbooks that paid no tax last month. Track lump deductions as their own item. Watch launches and relaunches. Look into the commercial months with no tax in 2024 and 2025. Do not set a cap from these numbers alone: solving the prescriptive problem needs free play issued by month and evidence on how wagering responds when the deduction is limited.

Testing the forecasts on months the models have not seen, and using methods built for a yes-or-no target, belongs to BUAN 381.
